What model type is most popular, and which engine can serve it?

Based on the live OpenRouter catalog sorted by weekly tokens processed, openrouter.ai/models?order=top-weekly (ranking and per-model token volumes from the top-weekly API, 759 models). Snapshot: May 2026. Built for the infaas serving stack (vLLM + Triton).

Bottom line: the catalog is overwhelmingly text generation models, and the highest-usage ones are all the same shape: decoder-only, Mixture-of-Experts transformer LLMs built for coding and agentic work. For that dominant class, vLLM is the right single engine: 199 of the top 200 open models are vLLM targets (the one exception outputs multiple modalities and routes to Triton). It does not cover image, audio, speech or video models, so a complete platform pairs vLLM with a multi-backend server like Triton.

3. Can one engine serve all of them?

No single engine covers every modality. But coverage maps cleanly onto the category counts from section 1.

Modality (count)Example modelsBest self-host enginevLLM?
Text generation (675)DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, MiMo, GLM, MiniMax, LlamavLLM (also SGLang, TensorRT-LLM)Yes
Embeddings (27)Qwen3 Embedding, Gemini EmbeddingvLLM or TEI / TritonYes

Transcription / ASR (9)Parakeet TDT, Qwen3 ASR, VoxtralNVIDIA NeMo / Riva / TritonNo
Speech / TTS (9)Grok Voice TTSspecialized / TritonNo
Image generation (30)Recraft, Grok Imaginediffusers / ComfyUI / TritonNo
Video (14)Grok Imagine Videospecialized / TritonNo

Where vLLM wins

  • Purpose-built for decoder-only causal LMs, dense and MoE.
  • PagedAttention, continuous batching, tensor and pipeline parallelism.
  • FP8 / AWQ / GPTQ quantization, speculative decoding.
  • Covers the entire Text category, ~87% of models and the overwhelming majority of tokens.

Where vLLM stops

  • No image, audio, speech, or video generation.
  • Brand-new architectures need explicit modeling code, so day-one support is not guaranteed.
  • GGUF / CPU / edge serving is not its focus (use llama.cpp).
  • Classic CV / classification / ONNX / TensorRT models are out of scope (use Triton).

Why vLLM and Triton dominate

  • vLLM supports every decoder-only and MoE family in the list, including DeepSeek sparse attention and Nemotron's hybrid Mamba-Transformer.
  • Triton serves all of them by wrapping vLLM (or TensorRT-LLM) as a backend, then adds non-LLM modalities on top.

Why the others fall short

  • llama.cpp: needs GGUF conversion, drops the multimodal path, struggles with hybrid Mamba (Nemotron), and the largest MoE checkpoints (1.6T-param DeepSeek V4 Pro) are impractical.
  • TensorRT-LLM / SGLang: strong on mainstream decoders, but multimodal and brand-new variants lag vLLM's day-one support.

6. Recommendation for infaas

  • vLLM is the primary engine. It serves the model type that dominates usage: open-weight decoder-only MoE text LLMs. 199 of the top 200 open models are vLLM targets.
  • Triton is the catch-all second engine. A multi-backend router (ONNX, TensorRT, PyTorch, TF, Python, and a vLLM backend) that absorbs everything vLLM cannot: ASR, TTS, image, video, rerank, classic non-LLM models.
  • Proprietary frontier models are out of reach for any self-hosted engine. Claude, Gemini, GPT, Grok, Qwen-Max and Tencent Hy3 are API-only by design, so they are a routing/proxy concern, not a serving one.
